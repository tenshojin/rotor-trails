# Rotor Trails

[简体中文](README.md) | **English** | [日本語](README.ja.md)

A first-person helicopter sim that runs in your browser. Nothing to download or install: open the page and fly. A bay full of islands in Japan, the canyons of the American Southwest, the Amazon rainforest, a Norwegian fjord, the snows of the Himalaya, and the Grand Canyon built from real survey data.

**Play it here: https://tenshojin.github.io/rotor-trails/**

On a computer you fly with keyboard and mouse, or with a gamepad (Xbox / PlayStation layout). On a phone or tablet, turn the device sideways and fly with two on-screen sticks (Full Sim is computer-only). Desktop Chrome or Edge with hardware acceleration turned on is recommended. Your progress is saved in your own browser. The game is available in English, Japanese and Simplified Chinese; it follows your browser's language, and you can switch at the top right of the main menu. If this is your first time, start with Flight School.

## What you can do

- **Missions**: five maps of transport flights, airdrops, inspections and precision landings. There are alternative routes to choose from and optional objectives. Nothing is timed; you are scored on how well you fly.
- **Free Flight**: no missions, no timer, and a crash is not a failure. Pick a time of day and the weather and start in the air. You can change the time and weather mid-flight or jump to a sight. Press J and the aircraft flies itself from sight to sight; press V for the cinematic camera and just watch. When you reach a place, its name and one line about it fade in. The game remembers where you have been, and the pause menu shows which places you haven't visited and which way they are. Click one and the aircraft flies there by itself.
- **Flight School**: two courses. Basic Flight has 7 lessons, starting with simply looking around and teaching one move at a time. Full Sim has 8 lessons, from a cold start all the way to an engine failure in flight. If you crash, you go back to the start of the lesson. It is not a failure.
- **Photo Mode**: open it from the pause menu. The world freezes, the camera goes anywhere, and you can adjust focal length, exposure, tilt and background blur, choose a film, warm, cool or monochrome look, and even hide the helicopter to shoot the landscape alone. Press F to save the picture, with no interface on it.
- **Replay**: available from the pause menu, the results screen and the failure screen. The cinematic camera plays back your last four minutes. You can slow it down, switch cameras, drag the timeline, and enter Photo Mode on any frame.
- **Logbook**: on the main menu. See how long you have flown, how many safe landings you have made, your softest touchdown and how many places you have visited, with 13 medals to earn.
- **Credits**: after the last map, or from About: an auto-cruise flight over Seto Bay at dusk while the credits roll, and you can keep flying when they end.
- **Full Sim**: no flight assists at all, and 135 buttons and switches that are all real and all clickable.

## Features

- **It flies with weight**: lift tilts with the airframe, and speeding up, slowing down and turning all take time and space. The higher you climb, the thinner the air and the tighter the power margin.
- **Assists are your choice**: Beginner, Standard and Challenge. Assists only help steady the aircraft; they never cancel inertia or wind. Hover hold (H) is off by default, and in Beginner mode the game suggests it when it sees you struggling to hold still. If you want to do everything yourself, choose Full Sim.
- **A living world**: vultures circle along the cliffs, gulls wheel over the harbor, parrots cross the rainforest in pairs, and they all move aside when you come close. Ferries and fishing boats trail white wakes across the sea, river boats work up and down the Amazon, and a light aircraft practices circuits over the training field. Fly close to a rock wall and the sound of your rotor bounces back off it.
- **The wind is real**: steady wind, gusts, wind channels in the canyons, updrafts and sinking air all push the aircraft around. Windsocks, flags, prayer flags, trees and the slant of the rain tell you where it is coming from. A windsock even tells you how strong it is: each inflated section is roughly 3 knots, and fully horizontal means 15 knots (about 8 m/s) or more. The orange signal smoke at your objective drifts with the wind too, leaning further the harder it blows. In strong wind the sea breaks into whitecaps, and once the surface is ruffled the reflections of distant islands dissolve into sky. Only on calm days is it a mirror.
- **The rotor makes wind**: fly low and the trees, bushes and grass are blown outward in waves, and water and dust are kicked up. In ordinary wind, big trees sway slowly while small trees and bushes move faster, the treetops bend more than the trunks, and the branches and leaves each shiver on their own.
- **Weather and time of day**: clear, rain, rainstorm, dense fog, low cloud, light snow, and clear turning to a rainstorm; day, dawn, dusk and night. Rain slants with the wind, lightning strikes the ground in a storm and the thunder arrives seconds later. At dawn mist lies in the valleys, after rain the roofs and the ground are wet, and in snow the ground, roofs and treetops slowly turn white. At night there are stars, the Milky Way, moonlight and the occasional meteor, an aurora over the Norwegian fjord, and the glow of the instrument backlights in the cabin.
- **Scenery worth a wallpaper**: rock walls weathered layer by layer, water that mirrors the mountains and trees, sunlight breaking through clouds and forest in visible shafts, and eyes that slowly adapt when you look from bright to dark.
- **A cockpit you can look at and touch**: analog gauges, with live engine, map and attitude displays. Turn your head to see the seats, the doors and the rear cabin.
- **Sound**: rotor, engine, wind and warnings are all synthesized in real time and follow rotor speed and airflow. The main menu and Free Flight have generated background music, which you can turn off in Settings.
- **Durability and landing checks**: a damaged aircraft gradually loses performance but always stays controllable. Hitting something raises dust, splashes water and throws debris; a damaged aircraft trails smoke and the windshield can crack. A landing is judged on descent rate, drift, attitude and position.
- **If you can see it, you can hit it**: the ferries and fishing boats in the bay, cars on the road, the small plane circling the airfield, plus street lights, floodlight masts, flagpoles, windsock poles, parked cars and the beams of a torii gate. Touch any of them and you take damage, and that includes a blade clipping a thin pole.
- **Cargo you delivered**: the crates you unload pile up at the edge of the pad.
- **You can't get stuck**: continue from the latest checkpoint or an earlier safe one, or use “Safe recovery” to return to a stable hover.
- **Pickups along the way**: power boost, shield and repair kits.
- **Graphics**: Low, Medium, High and Ultra, chosen automatically for your graphics card the first time you play. Higher quality means you see further: on Ultra, trees are drawn out to 6 km, and terrain and building detail and shadows reach further too. There is also a ray tracing switch, which lets shaded rock walls pick up warm light from the opposite side and the belly of the aircraft catch light bounced off the ground.
- **Your save is yours**: progress lives in your browser, and you can export it to a file and import it on another computer or browser (Settings → Assists & display).

### The Grand Canyon

This map is not generated by an algorithm: every peak and every layer of rock is where it is in reality. The elevation comes from U.S. Geological Survey data, the surface is aerial imagery from 2023, and the trees on the South Rim were identified one by one from that imagery.

It is a huge map: all 41 × 41 km can be flown, from Mather Point on the South Rim down to the Colorado River, and on to the North Rim lodge, Cape Royal and Hermits Rest. The central 10 km is the most detailed, and when you fly low there you can make out trails, road markings and the cars in the parking lots (30 cm aerial imagery, downloaded as you fly; not used on Low quality). Further out, the ground is softer when you fly right down on it. The first time you open this map it downloads about 90 MB.

## Controls

The gamepad layout is listed under “Controls” in the game. Pick up a gamepad and the on-screen hints and tutorials switch to gamepad buttons; if the gamepad disconnects in flight, the game pauses. In menus, the right stick scrolls long pages. In Photo Mode, D-pad left and right pick a setting such as focal length or exposure, and up and down adjust it. In a replay, A pauses and LB / RB skip back and forward 5 seconds. With a gamepad in Full Sim, a crosshair sits in the middle of the screen: aim at a switch with the right stick, A flips it forward and X flips it back, hold to turn a knob, and click the left stick to trim.

Phones and tablets: a small panel on the left shows ground speed, height above ground and airframe; the hover and cruise buttons light up while they are on, and the boost button is dim when you have no power boost; at a fork in a mission, tap a route card to choose the suggested route. Safari on iPhone can't make a web page fullscreen, so use Share → “Add to Home Screen”, and the game opens fullscreen in landscape from there.

During a flight, the browser's Back (the back button on a phone, a mouse side button) pauses the game instead of leaving the page. On a computer, Settings → Graphics has a fullscreen button.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W / S | Pitch forward to speed up / pitch back to slow down |
| A / D | Turn left / right |
| Space | Climb |
| Shift | Descend |
| Q / E | Slide left / right |
| H | Hover hold: slows down gradually and holds position |
| C | Power boost |
| Mouse | Look around (does not steer the aircraft) |
| Z / V | Recenter view / switch camera (Free Flight only; missions and training stay in the cockpit) |
| N / M | Switch navigation target / cockpit map range |
| U | HUD: auto, full, hidden |
| F | Screenshot: saves this frame without the HUD, even while paused. To choose your angle, use Photo Mode from the pause menu |
| J | Auto-cruise (Free Flight): flies itself from sight to sight; touch any flight control to take over |
| Esc or P | Pause |

Every key can be changed under Settings → Controls.

## Full Sim

Choose “Full Sim” under “Flight assists” in a mission briefing, or under “Flight model” in Free Flight. If you are new to it, take Flight School → Full Sim first, which takes about 20 minutes.

- **Cold start**: the aircraft is powered down. Work through the checklist at the top left step by step, or click “Do it for me”.
- **The mouse is your eyes and fingers**: move it to the edge of the screen to turn your head, faster the closer you get to the edge. Pointing at a control only shows its name and reading; clicking is what presses it. Left-click to operate, right-click to go back one position, and use the wheel to turn knobs.
- **No flight assists**: you watch the rotor RPM yourself, an engine failure leaves you only autorotation, descending too fast puts you into vortex ring state, overspeed brings retreating blade stall, and over-torque damages the main gearbox.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W / S | Cyclic forward / back |
| A / D | Pedals at low speed; mostly bank once you have speed |
| Q / E | Cyclic left / right |
| Space / Shift | Collective up / down (it stays where you leave it) |
| C | Power boost |
| T | Trim: makes the current stick position the place it returns to when released |
| H | Stability augmentation (SAS) on / off |

**The aircraft always wanting to turn right is real physics, not a bug.** Seen from above, the main rotor turns counterclockwise. The engine twists the rotor to the left, so the rotor twists the fuselage to the right. This is the torque reaction. With the SAS on, it holds the nose for you; with it off, you have to tap A for left pedal yourself. A hover takes about 10%, and the pedal stays where you put it.

Windows “Sticky Keys” pops up when Shift is pressed five times in a row. If you descend with Shift a lot, you can turn its shortcut off under Windows Settings → Accessibility → Keyboard.
